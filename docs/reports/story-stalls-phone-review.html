How it happens

The scheduler can run out of work it is allowed to claim while blocked stories still exist. A finished run can therefore be mistaken for a finished backlog.

Current impact: Individual dependencies or a whole empty run; independent eligible graph work can still proceed.

Current behavior — source snapshot

Independent graph work remains eligible. A run can finish with blocked work remaining. Strict priority has no cross-priority aging.

Proposed disposition — scope and independent work

Hold affected story/subgraph; dispatch independent eligible work under existing policy.

Recommended handling — proposed policy

R0 on the exact prerequisite; report executable/held/cyclic/reserved/owned counts and “no executable work,” separately from “all complete.” Preserve graph and priority.

Alternatives & tradeoffs

Recommended handling above

Benefit: Make the unresolved prerequisite visible without changing priority.

Cost / limitation: Requires accurate waiting categories and bypass-age reporting.

Conservative alternative

Keep the existing run result and inspect blockers manually.

Benefit: No scheduler behavior changes.

Cost / limitation: The person must distinguish completion from an empty eligible queue.

Editorial comparison. Neither option grants authority to bypass the safeguards below.

Escalation and proof that recovery is complete

Name cycle/out-of-scope/free-text prerequisite or bypass age to owner. Complete when dependency closes or an authorized graph/state change makes work eligible.

Policy or design choice to review: Review whether a future fairness quota is needed; never silently raise story priority.

How it happens

Turning automation off records the setting, then waits for already admitted operations to release their permits. That final wait can have no outer deadline.

Current impact: The project control response can remain waiting while admitted effects drain. This does not mean the setting failed to change.

Current behavior — source snapshot

Setting persists as disabled; verifier work is cancelled; provider sessions are preserved. The outer condition-variable wait has no timeout.

Proposed disposition — scope and independent work

Manual work and independent projects may continue, subject to actual custody. No automatic replay when reenabled.

Recommended handling — proposed policy

Acknowledge the setting change within a bounded control budget and expose “disabled; admitted effects draining.” Observe owner progress through RO; never declare the drain complete early.

What the retry codes mean

These numerical timings are proposals, not measured optima. An existing stricter domain policy takes precedence. Manual mode does not gain automatic retry authority.

R0 — prerequisite hold

Zero unchanged effect retries. Wake on a relevant dependency, exact head/base, credential/capability, review, label, policy or owner change. One coalesced notice; remain visible without repeated comment/notification writes.

RO — observation

One owner; read-only observations after 30, 60 and 120 seconds, then at most every five minutes or on a relevant event. Escalate after the initial bounded attempt set. Continuing passive observation does not grant mutation authority or clear custody. Respect server Retry-After and coalesce shared failures. These times are proposals, not measured optima.

RT — proven transient failure

Preserve the current verifier's three-total-attempt, 30-second-spacing ceiling where applicable. For new effect retries, require proof the prior effect did not occur or has settled; cap at one retry after 30 seconds unless an existing domain policy is stricter. Permanent auth, policy, integrity and causal failures use R0. No budget reset from an acknowledgement or restart.

RC — cleanup/reconciliation

Use the existing owner and its exact process/group/lease postconditions. Preserve its deadline across retries; do not reset the owned operation's clock. Retry idempotent reconciliation only after the previous controller has settled, at most three reconciliation attempts after 30/120/300 seconds (or a stricter existing policy), then attention while custody stays fenced. Never reap foreign processes or release on age alone.

RQ — resource queue

Keep one durable request ID and fairness age; use broker/holder events and the existing observation cadence. Do not spawn competing requests. Separate queue residence from execution timeout. An optional residence budget parks the logical job while retaining its queue identity and all physical custody.
